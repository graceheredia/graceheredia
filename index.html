Project 1 Pre-writing Tree

My phenomenon is tension between parents and ADHD medication. Other potential phenomena that I could have chosen include how people conform to their environments, internal hyperactivity within inattentive ADHD, and younger generations facing pressure about being medicated for ADHD. I chose the tension with parents specifically because I face this problem every day, and I highly relate to it. Conformity was a broad phenomenon that I noticed in my inquiries, but when I looked deeply into them, I saw a specific pattern. My thoughts were constantly changing, and I was unable to make consistent observations for me to even see a pattern, most likely because of my ADHD. This led me to internal hyperactivity within inattentive ADHD. I still felt like it wasn’t specific enough, though, and it would be hard for me to find sources about it. I eventually came down to tension between parents and ADHD medication, mostly due to my lived experiences rather than my observations. My observations allowed me to see how my brain attempted to make clear and deep thoughts, but they weren’t thorough enough. Seeing my disorder in my writing made me recognize that I could choose a phenomenon based on what I experience living with.

I narrowed down my options to medication because it relates to what we have been going over in class. I saw a pattern of stigma with my medication and ultimately related it to what I personally struggle with, which is trying to relieve the tension between being medicated and my parents’ opposition to it. I interpret my phenomenon to be an annoying struggle, but it’s normal for me. I never thought too deeply into it because I assumed that most people’s parents wouldn’t want them on medication either. That made me wonder: How do other people’s parents feel about it? What kind of struggles do they face? Are they subtle, or do they interfere with life dramatically? For me, the tension is subtle; that is why I never noticed it.

Some cultural artifacts that relate to my phenomenon include a Netflix documentary, Take Your Pills; a South Park episode, “Timmy 2000”; and the CIBA 1970 Ritalin advertisement. I believe one of the strongest cultural artifacts is the Take Your Pills documentary because it heavily creates a stigma toward ADHD medications, showing the “abusive” side of them. I actually watched this documentary with my mom, and it came with a lot of tension between us. The documentary shows a lot more of the negative side of the medication and very little of the positive. While watching it with her, she continuously made negative comments toward me because I was medicated, trying to make me change my mind about it. Because the documentary focuses more on how people abuse ADHD medications to enhance their performance, it undermines the actual medical use for them.

It can expand my current interpretation to include parents who may believe that ADHD medications are abused so much that they may fear putting their child on them. It can complicate my interpretation by showing that they may feel like bad parents for putting their child on this medication because the drug is marketed as something that helps children perform as well as others in school rather than as a medical necessity. While this could be about parents not seeing the actual medical necessity for the medication, it could also be about their fears of having their child depend on a drug to function for the rest of their life. The strong stigma around the medication may also make them feel like bad parents for complying with treatment.
